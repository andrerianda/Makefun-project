You are a vehicle artist on MARAZUL. Read /tmp/marazul/briefs/ART-RULES.md in full first: it gives the project, the rules you must not break, the pipeline and what to report. Then read art/README.md and the example asset script.

## Your job: the everyday cars
Model, bake and ship these seven road cars as game-ready .glb assets, with exactly these asset ids: compact_hatch, family_sedan, taxi_cab, police_cruiser, luxury_sedan, suv, police_suv. They are what fills the streets as traffic and what the police drive, so they appear in every screenshot, usually several at once, from a chase camera a few metres behind and from the pavement. They must read as real modern cars: correct stance and proportions (wheelbase, overhangs, greenhouse, wheel size against body), continuous curved body surfaces with crisp character lines, visible panel gaps for doors, hood and trunk, wheel arches with depth, real wheels (tyre with sidewall and tread shoulder, alloy or steel rim with spokes, brake disc behind), headlamp and tail-lamp units with lenses and inner detail, grille, bumpers, mirrors, door handles, wipers, exhaust tips, window frames and a simple dark interior seen through the glass (seats, dashboard, steering wheel, door cards). No toy shapes: no boxes with rounded corners.

You own: art/blender/assets/vehicles/** (asset scripts and your underscore-prefixed shared helpers), game/assets/vehicles/**, game/src/assets/registry/vehicles.js. Scratch: /tmp/marazul/vehicles/.

Work in this order. First build your shared car kit as helper modules (a body builder that lofts a car body from side, top and section profiles so each car is a set of parameters and curves rather than a new program; wheels and tyres; lamps; glass; mirrors; interior; underbody; the standard nodes, anchors and colliders), prove it on family_sedan until that car looks right in Cycles and in the engine viewer, and only then make the others. taxi_cab and police_cruiser are their own designs built from the kit (they may share a platform with a sedan but need their own details: a roof sign for the taxi; for police a roof light bar, push bar, spotlight, steel wheels); police_suv relates to suv the same way.

## Contract with the game (fixed; other agents code against it)
- Dimensions are real: for example compact_hatch about 4.0 x 1.75 x 1.48 m, family_sedan 4.8 x 1.85 x 1.45, luxury_sedan 5.2 x 1.9 x 1.47, suv 4.9 x 1.95 x 1.75. If game/src/catalog/vehicleCatalog.js exists when you start a car, take its dimensions, seats and typical colours from there.
- Origin on the ground at the centre of the wheelbase, facing forward (see the conventions).
- Separate nodes: wheel_fl, wheel_fr, wheel_rl, wheel_rr (origin at the hub, spinning about local X; the front ones are steered by the game about the vertical axis through the hub, so give them clearance); door_fl and door_fr (origin on the hinge line at the front edge, glass and door card move with them); hood and trunk (origin on the hinge); bumper_f and bumper_r (the game detaches them in crashes); steering_wheel (origin at its hub, its local Z in Blender pointing along the column toward the driver, the game turns it about that axis); window_front, window_rear, window_l, window_r (the glass panes not in doors, so the game can shatter them). Everything else is the body.
- Anchors: anchor_seat_driver and anchor_seat_passenger_1..3 (where the seated person's hip joints are, pointing the way they face); anchor_door_driver and anchor_door_passenger (on the ground where a person stands to get in, facing the door); anchor_light_head_l/r and anchor_light_tail_l/r; anchor_exhaust_1 (and _2 if twin); anchor_camera (chase camera target, about roof height at the centre); anchor_plate_f and anchor_plate_r (centre of the licence plate recess, facing outward; the game paints plates); anchor_decal_door_l and anchor_decal_door_r (centre of the front door skin, facing outward; the game paints liveries and lettering there); anchor_sign_roof on the taxi; anchor_siren on police vehicles.
- Materials by role: paint for the body colour (recoloured by the game, with clear coat), paint_secondary where a livery needs a second colour (police doors and roof, taxi band); glass for windows and lamp lenses that must be transparent; lamp_head, lamp_tail, lamp_brake, lamp_reverse, lamp_indicator_l, lamp_indicator_r; on police vehicles lamp_siren_a and lamp_siren_b (the two colours of the light bar); on the taxi lamp_sign. Everything else bakes into the atlas (tyres, rims, grille, trim, lamp interiors, the interior, the underbody) with ambient occlusion.
- Colliders: one col_box_body for the lower body and one col_box_cabin for the greenhouse; no wheel colliders.
- Levels of detail: lod0 for the player's car and near traffic (about 14,000 to 18,000 triangles including wheels and interior), lod1 about 4,000 to 5,000, lod2 about 800 to 1,200 with no interior and simple wheels. The body mesh of lod0 should be evenly tessellated (no edge longer than about 0.3 m) because the game deforms it in crashes. Keep the draw calls of lod2 low.
- Size: at most about 480 kB per car and 3.4 MiB for all seven; set ASSET['budget'] accordingly.

In the registry, document for each car its nodes, anchors, materials and the wheel radius and track the physics needs. In your report give a table of wheel radius, wheelbase, track width and seat count per car.
