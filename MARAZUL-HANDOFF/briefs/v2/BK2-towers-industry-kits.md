## Your job: the skyline and the working city
The city is about 3.4 by 3.0 km with thousands of buildings, so buildings are not modelled one by one: a later programmer assembles each building on its lot from facade modules you make, instancing them by the thousand. You design and bake the kits for the downtown towers that make the skyline (seen from the beach, the bridges and the bay in almost every wide screenshot, glowing at dusk) and for the port, industrial and roadside city. In low golden sunlight towers must show real structure: curtain walls with mullions, spandrels and reflective glass that picks up the sky, concrete frames with deep-set windows, balconies with glass balustrades on residential towers, podiums and lobbies with tall glazing and canopies, mechanical floors with louvres, crowns, roof plant; and the working buildings must show corrugated cladding with ridges, roller doors, loading docks with bumpers and levellers, pipe runs, ducts, ladders, skylights, rust and stains. Baked ambient occlusion and normal detail carry it at distance.

You own: art/blender/assets/towers_industry/** (asset scripts and your underscore-prefixed shared helpers), game/assets/towers_industry/**, and a new registry module game/src/assets/registry/towersAndIndustry.js (add its one line to registry/index.js as the rules describe). Scratch: /tmp/marazul/towers/. Another artist is making the low and mid-rise street kits in art/blender/assets/buildings/ to the same module grid; do not write there.

## The module grid (fixed; both building artists and the assembler use it)
- A module is one bay of one storey of a facade. In Blender it is authored facing +Y (the street side), with the outer wall surface on the plane Y = 0, spanning X from -width/2 to +width/2 and Z from 0 to its height. Parts may project up to 1.6 m toward the street (canopies, balconies) and recess up to 0.7 m into the building. The back is open; the assembler closes buildings with plain walls and roofs.
- Widths: 4 m (standard bay), 2 m (narrow bay), 8 m (wide lobby, dock or garage bay). The assembler may stretch a module by up to 12 percent in width to fit a frontage. Heights: ground storey 4.2 m (towers may add a double-height lobby module of 8.4 m), upper storeys 3.2 m for residential and 4.0 m for office towers and industrial sheds (say which in each kit), top pieces (parapets, crowns, roof edges) 1.2 m, also at widths 4 and 2.
- Corners: a corner module is a square post 0.5 by 0.5 m in plan, one storey high, with its two street faces on X = +0.25 and Y = 0, placed where two facades meet; glass towers also get a glazed corner.
- Slots (the vocabulary the assembler understands): ground_shop, ground_shop_wide, ground_entrance (the main door or lobby), ground_wall, ground_garage (vehicle entrance, roller door or loading dock), upper_window, upper_balcony, upper_blank, upper_mechanical (louvred plant floors), top_parapet, top_cornice, top_crown (tower tops), corner_ground, corner_upper, corner_top, and roof_prop (free-standing roof equipment: cooling towers, chillers, lift overruns, window-cleaning cradles, helipad markings frame, masts with aviation lights, big ventilators, tanks, pipe racks, chimneys; origin at the centre of the base).
- Naming: each kit is one asset, kit_<style>; each module in it is a node named <slot>_<width in metres>_<variant letter>, for example upper_window_4_a, with its levels as children or suffixes exactly as the pipeline's level convention requires (read art/README.md). Record each module's width, height and slot in its glTF extras if the pipeline lets you, and in any case in the registry entry.
- Levels: lod0 is the detailed module (about 100 to 400 triangles for a bay, up to 900 for a lobby or dock). lod1 is essential, because the skyline shows tens of thousands of bays: at most 8 triangles, a flat card on the wall plane whose texture is baked from the detailed module (base colour, normal, occlusion, roughness, metalness and the window glow), so that distant towers still show their grid of windows with depth and reflect the sky.
- Materials by role: paint_wall for the main solid finish (concrete, panel or cladding baked neutral with its texture, occlusion and weathering, tinted per building by the game), paint_trim for the accent (mullion caps, bands, doors), lamp_window_a, lamp_window_b and lamp_window_c for glazing: the game lights a random share at night by group, and by day they must read as reflective glass (metalness near 1, roughness under 0.1, a mid-tone tinted base colour: near-black glass reflects nothing), with a hint of floor edges, blinds and ceiling lights behind; spread the three groups evenly over the modules and within the big curtain-wall bays. Lobby glazing is glass role only where seeing in matters (then put a simple baked lobby behind it). Aviation and crown lights are lamp_beacon and lamp_crown. Everything else bakes into the kit's atlas. No lettering anywhere: give each lobby, shopfront, dock and tower crown an anchor, anchor_sign_<module name>, at the centre of its sign area facing the street with the usable width and height as extras, and each wall or dock lamp an anchor_light.
- Each kit also carries the repeating tile materials the assembler needs to close the building: tile_wall (the same finish as paint_wall, for side and back walls), tile_roof_flat, and for the sheds tile_roof_metal (ribbed sheet), each about 2 m square at 512 pixels (4 m for curtain wall if you provide tile_curtain_wall as a cheap infill for the far sides of towers).

## The kits to ship (asset ids)
1. kit_glass_tower: modern office and hotel towers of 15 to 45 storeys: several curtain-wall bay designs (flush glass, expressed mullions, horizontal bands with spandrels, vertical fins), a podium with lobby and shops, mechanical floors, set-back crowns and a spire or mast base. Glass tints are set by the game through paint_trim and the glass base colour; say how.
2. kit_residential_tower: white and pastel concrete apartment towers of 10 to 30 storeys by the bay: deep balconies with glass or railing balustrades, sliding doors, sun-shade slabs, rounded balcony corners, penthouse tops, a porte-cochere entrance.
3. kit_concrete_civic: mid-century concrete and stone civic and office buildings of 3 to 12 storeys (the police headquarters, hospital, city hall, courthouse, bank and parking garages are assembled from it): deep window grids, brise-soleil, colonnades and grand stairs' frontage modules, open parking-deck bays with ramps' edges and barriers, flag-pole and emblem anchors.
4. kit_industrial_port: warehouses, factories, workshops, the port and roadside sheds, 1 to 3 storeys of 4.0 m: corrugated metal and painted block walls, roller doors, loading docks, office windows, saw-tooth and ribbed roof edges, pipe bridges, silos' and tanks' wall segments, chain-link fence panels and gates with barbed wire (as cutout role), and as free-standing pieces in the roof_prop slot: a shipping container in 6 m and 12 m lengths (tinted through paint_wall), a stack-ready design; a dockside gantry crane too large for the grid is not part of the kit.
Each kit: 25 to 40 modules, one 2048 atlas (shared by all its modules) plus its tile sets, at most about 0.9 MiB, 3.6 MiB for all four; set ASSET['budget'] with the reason (a whole kit in one file). Build kit_glass_tower first and prove it by assembling, in a scratch Blender scene and in the engine viewer if it can show it, a thirty-storey tower with podium and crown from its modules, lit by a low warm sun against a sky, once with lod0 near the ground and once entirely from lod1 cards as it would be seen from a kilometre away; iterate until both pictures look like a real tower, then build the others the same way.

In the registry, list every module with slot, width and height, the anchors, and the material roles; that table is the assembler's only documentation. Repeat the list of modules per kit in your report, with the paths of the assembled-building pictures.
