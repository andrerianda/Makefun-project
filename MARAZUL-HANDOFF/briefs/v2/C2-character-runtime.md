## The contract with the artist
- Every person uses one skeleton with these bones: root (on the ground between the feet), hips, spine, chest, neck, head, and left and right shoulder, upper_arm, forearm, hand, thigh, shin, foot, toe (names end in _l or _r), plus an optional jaw. Bones run from joint to joint. Proportions differ between people (1.55 to 1.95 m; slim to heavy), so read every length from the rest joint positions in the loaded file and never hard-code one body. Do not depend on bone roll: derive each bone's rest orientation from the file and express poses as rotations relative to rest in the character's own space (forward -Z, up +Y, right +X).
- Rest pose: an A-pose, arms straight about 40 degrees below horizontal, legs straight, feet hip-width apart.
- Anchors on bones: anchor_hand_r, anchor_hand_l (in the palm, pointing along a held pistol's barrel), anchor_head, anchor_back, anchor_hip_r. Levels of detail are separate skinned meshes (lod0, lod1, lod2) on the same skeleton. Materials named paint_skin, paint_hair, paint_top, paint_bottom, paint_shoes, paint_jacket, paint_accent are recoloured per person.
- Vehicles give you anchor_seat_driver and anchor_seat_passenger_N (where the seated person's hip joints go, pointing the way they face), a steering_wheel node, anchor_door_driver (where to stand to get in), and on motorcycles anchor_grip_l/r and anchor_foot_l/r.
- The first real body will appear under game/assets/characters/ with its entry in game/src/assets/registry/characters.js, perhaps an hour from now. Until then develop on a stand-in you build in code under tools/preview/ (never shipped in game/): the same bone hierarchy with plausible rest positions for a 1.78 m adult in the A-pose, drawn with capsules and boxes for limbs, torso, head, hands and feet so that poses are easy to judge. Switch your preview to the real body as soon as it exists, check the motions on it (and on a second body of different size when there is one), and fix what looks wrong.

## What to build
A. The rig layer: wraps an instantiated character asset (or the stand-in), maps the bones, measures the body, and offers fast pose writing; two-bone inverse kinematics for arms and legs with pole control and soft limits; look-at for head and eyes line; spine twist and lean distribution; foot planting on uneven ground (the caller supplies a ground-height function) with pelvis adjustment; hand targets in another object's space (a steering wheel, handlebars, a weapon).
B. A procedural motion library, each motion a small module that writes a pose from a phase or a time and a few parameters, tuned until it looks human: standing idle with breathing and weight shifts (several variants so a crowd does not move in step); walking, jogging, running and sprinting from one gait model whose stride length, cadence, arm swing, pelvis rotation, bounce, lean and foot roll change with speed, with no foot sliding at any speed or body size; turning on the spot; strafing and walking backwards; crouch and crouch-walk; jump, fall and landing; vaulting a low obstacle; climbing out of water and a swimming stroke with treading water; getting into a car (open door, sit, close) and out again, and being thrown out; sitting in a car and driving with hands that follow the steering wheel, a passenger pose, leaning in corners, looking back when reversing, driving and shooting; riding a motorcycle (hands on grips, feet on pegs, lean); standing at a boat's helm; unarmed combat (guard stance, jab, cross, hook, kick, a three-hit combination, block, being hit from four directions, stagger, knock-down and getting up); aiming and firing with one-handed and two-handed pistols, a submachine gun, a shotgun and a rifle (upper-body aim that follows a target direction with spine twist, recoil, reload, lowering the weapon, running with it), throwing; hands up in surrender, kneeling for arrest, being handcuffed; pedestrians' reactions (flinch, cower, crouch behind hands, panic run with flailing arms, pointing, shouting, phoning, filming with a phone, cheering); everyday life (talking with gestures, listening, using a phone, smoking, leaning on a wall, sitting on a bench, sunbathing on a towel, dancing in two or three styles, carrying a bag or a box, sweeping, a street vendor's wave, a police officer directing traffic, a paramedic kneeling by a patient); death falls in several directions; and a light physical ragdoll for crashes and explosions (points and constraints over the skeleton, collision against a supplied ground function and boxes, blending back into the get-up motion).
C. An animation controller per character: layers (full body, upper body, additive, face and jaw), smooth cross-fades with inertia so nothing pops, a locomotion input (desired velocity, facing, stance, mode: on foot, swimming, driving, riding), one-shot actions with events (a punch lands, a shot fires, a foot hits the ground, a door opens) that game systems subscribe to, and a small declarative table that maps game states to motions so other programmers never touch bone maths. Deterministic for a given seed.
D. Crowd performance: sixty characters animated at 60 frames per second on a mid-range machine; update rates and motion detail that fall with distance and stop off screen; shared scratch memory; a cheap far mode. Measure it in Node and say what you measured.
E. Appearance at run time: applying a person's colours to the paint roles from an appearance record (the catalogs give skin tone, hair colour and outfit colours as hex), and attaching objects to anchors.
F. A preview page, tools/preview/characters.html (served from the repository root; sets window.__gameReady and window.__previewReady), that can show any motion at a chosen phase or a strip of phases across the picture, a sheet of many motions at once, a turntable, the stand-in or a real body, on a ground with the finished lighting (see tools/preview/atmosphere.html for how a preview uses the atmosphere), driven by query parameters. Use it to look at every motion as a strip of six to eight phases and correct what a person would not do: balance over the supporting foot, knees that bend the right way, elbows that do not cross the body, shoulders that move with the arms, heads that lead turns.
G. Node tests under tools/tests/characters/ as *.test.mjs (run with a quoted glob) for the inverse kinematics, the gait model (foot speed matches ground speed during stance at several speeds and body sizes), the controller's blending and events, and the ragdoll's stability.
