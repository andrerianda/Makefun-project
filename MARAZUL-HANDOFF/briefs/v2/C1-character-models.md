## Your job: the people
Build a parametric human generator in Blender Python and with it model, skin, bake and ship the game's people as rigged .glb assets: the two playable leads, the supporting cast, the pedestrians, the police and the gangs. The owner asked for realistic 3D people, not blocky figures. They are seen from a third-person camera three to five metres behind the lead, in crowds on the pavement, and closer in cutscenes, so they must read as real adults at every distance: true proportions (about seven and a half heads tall; shoulders, ribcage, waist, pelvis, limbs with muscle and joint shapes, not tubes), a real head (skull shape, brow, eye sockets with eyeballs, nose with nostrils, lips, chin, jaw, ears, neck), real hands (a thumb and four fingers, modelled in a relaxed half-closed grip that suits a steering wheel or a pistol; no finger bones), feet in shoes, hair with volume and a believable hairline (layered shells or cards), and clothes that are their own geometry with thickness at collars, hems, cuffs and waistbands, folds at elbows and knees baked into the normal map, seams, pockets, belts, buttons, soles. Skin, cloth, leather, denim and hair come from the material library and are baked with ambient occlusion.

You own: art/blender/assets/characters/** (asset scripts and your underscore-prefixed shared helpers), game/assets/characters/**, game/src/assets/registry/characters.js. Scratch: /tmp/marazul/characters/. A character programmer is writing the run-time animation in game/src/characters/ at the same time and is waiting for your first body: do not write there, and ship one complete lead (built, registered, tests passing) as early as you can, then tell nobody, just carry on; the programmer watches game/assets/characters/.

## Contract with the game (fixed; the programmer codes against it)
- One skeleton for everybody, with exactly the bone names and hierarchy of SKELETON_BONES in art/blender/lib/naming.py (root on the ground between the feet; hips; spine; chest; neck; head; shoulder, upper_arm, forearm, hand left and right; thigh, shin, foot, toe left and right). You may add one bone, jaw (child of head), for talking in cutscenes, and nothing else. Bones run from joint to joint. The run-time works out everything from the rest joint positions in the file, so body proportions may and should differ between people (heights from about 1.55 to 1.95 m, builds slim, average, athletic, heavy; male and female).
- Rest pose: standing upright, facing forward, feet hip-width apart and pointing forward, legs straight, arms straight and held about 40 degrees below horizontal out to the sides (an A-pose), palms facing down and slightly forward, head level.
- Skinning must survive the poses the game uses: arms down at the sides, arms forward on a steering wheel, arms raised overhead, deep sitting (hips and knees at 90 degrees and more), a full running stride, crouching, torso twist, head turned 80 degrees. Test those poses in Blender on your first body and fix the weights (shoulders, elbows, hips, knees, the crotch and the armpits are where it goes wrong), and include a posed contact sheet in your review. No animation clips are needed in the files; the game animates procedurally.
- Levels of detail are separate skinned meshes on the same skeleton: lod0 about 7,000 to 9,000 triangles, lod1 about 2,500, lod2 about 700. The pipeline's decimation of skinned meshes is untried; if it breaks weights, build the lower levels from the generator with coarser settings instead.
- Anchors, parented to bones: anchor_hand_r and anchor_hand_l (in the palm, pointing where a held pistol's barrel points when the arm is extended), anchor_head (top of the head, for hats and markers), anchor_back (between the shoulder blades, for a slung weapon or backpack), anchor_hip_r (holster).
- Materials by role, so that the game makes many different people from each file by recolouring: paint_skin (baked as neutral mid skin with its shading detail, so a tint gives any complexion), paint_hair, paint_top, paint_bottom, paint_shoes, and where an outfit has one, paint_jacket or paint_accent. Eyes, teeth, lips tint, brows, buttons, belts, badges, jewellery and everything else bake into the atlas. Keep the number of materials per person at six or fewer.
- Appearance comes from the catalogs when they exist (a writer is producing game/src/catalog/storyCharacters.js, pedestrianArchetypes.js and gangsAndFactions.js right now, with build, height, skin tone, hair style and colour, outfit pieces and colours for each person). Start with the generator and a first lead from the list below, and when the catalogs appear make the story cast match them by their ids (asset id = character id).

## What to ship
About twenty-four files, in this order of importance:
1. The two playable leads (asset ids lead_a and lead_b until the catalog gives their ids; one man, one woman, late twenties to thirties, distinctive street clothes: the best two models in the set).
2. Eight to ten pedestrian bases covering the city: men and women, young to old, slim to heavy, in beach wear, casual, business, work wear and evening clothes, with different hair, so that recolouring the paint roles turns ten bases into a crowd (asset ids pedestrian_<short description>, for example pedestrian_tourist_m, pedestrian_office_f).
3. Uniformed: police_officer_m, police_officer_f, swat_officer (helmet, vest, pads), paramedic, and one gang_member base per gang style (street, biker, cartel-style suit) as the catalog describes them.
4. As many of the supporting cast as the budget allows, the ones the catalog marks as mission givers first.
Size: at most about 280 kB per person (a 1024 atlas, the leads may use 2048 for the base colour only if it shows) and 6.5 MiB in all; set ASSET['budget'] to match.

In the registry, document each person's bones, anchors, materials (with the default colour of each paint role) and height. In your report add the list of hair styles and outfit pieces the generator supports, so that the writer's catalog and the game can refer to them.
