You are a vehicle artist on MARAZUL. Read /tmp/marazul/briefs/ART-RULES.md in full first: it gives the project, the rules you must not break, the pipeline and what to report. Then read art/README.md and the example asset script.

## Your job: trucks, vans and the bus
Model, bake and ship these seven road vehicles as game-ready .glb assets, with exactly these asset ids: pickup_truck, offroad_4x4, cargo_van, ambulance, swat_van, box_truck, city_bus. They give the traffic its variety and scale and several are mission vehicles; the ambulance and the armoured SWAT van arrive with lights flashing when the player causes trouble. They must read as real vehicles: correct proportions and stance, body surfaces with character lines and panel gaps, wheel arches with depth, real wheels (tyres with sidewall and tread, steel or alloy rims, twin rear wheels on the box truck and bus), lamp units with lenses and inner detail, grilles, bumpers, big mirrors on arms, door handles, wipers, exhausts, steps and running boards, roof equipment (light bars, vents, air-conditioning pods), a ribbed or panelled cargo body with a roll-up or double rear door on the box truck and vans, a pickup bed with tailgate, big windows with rows of seats and grab rails on the bus, and a simple dark cab interior seen through the glass (seats, dashboard, steering wheel). No toy shapes.

You own: art/blender/assets/trucks/** (asset scripts and your underscore-prefixed shared helpers), game/assets/trucks/**, a new registry module game/src/assets/registry/trucks.js (add its one line to registry/index.js as the rules describe). Scratch: /tmp/marazul/trucks/. Other artists are building cars in art/blender/assets/vehicles/ and art/blender/assets/sports_cars/ at the same time; do not depend on those directories.

Work in this order. First write your shared helpers (cab and body builders driven by profiles and parameters; wheels and tyres in the sizes you need; lamps; glass; mirrors; cab interior; chassis and underbody; the standard nodes, anchors and colliders), prove them on pickup_truck until it looks right in Cycles and in the engine viewer, then make the others.

## Contract with the game (fixed; other agents code against it)
- Dimensions are real: for example pickup_truck about 5.6 x 2.0 x 1.9 m, offroad_4x4 4.6 x 1.9 x 1.9 (short, upright, big tyres, spare wheel on the back), cargo_van 5.6 x 2.0 x 2.4, ambulance 6.0 x 2.2 x 2.7 (van cab with a box body), swat_van 6.4 x 2.4 x 2.8 (armoured, slab-sided, small windows, roof hatch, side steps), box_truck 7.2 x 2.4 x 3.2, city_bus 12.0 x 2.55 x 3.1. If game/src/catalog/vehicleCatalog.js exists when you start a vehicle, take its dimensions, seats and typical colours from there.
- Origin on the ground at the centre of the wheelbase, facing forward (see the conventions).
- Separate nodes: wheel_fl, wheel_fr, wheel_rl, wheel_rr (origin at the hub, spinning about local X; twin rear wheels are one node per side; the front ones are steered by the game about the vertical axis through the hub, so give them clearance); door_fl and door_fr (origin on the hinge line at the front edge; on the bus door_fr is the front passenger door leaf); door_rear_l and door_rear_r on the vans and ambulance (the rear double doors, hinged at the outer edges), or door_rear as one roll-up panel on the box truck; hood where there is one; bumper_f and bumper_r (the game detaches them in crashes); steering_wheel (origin at its hub, its local Z in Blender pointing along the column toward the driver; the game turns it about that axis); window_front, window_rear, window_l, window_r (glass panes not in doors, so the game can shatter them). Everything else is the body.
- Anchors: anchor_seat_driver and anchor_seat_passenger_1 (plus up to _3; on the bus up to _6 spread along the cabin), marking where the seated person's hip joints are and pointing the way they face; anchor_door_driver and anchor_door_passenger (on the ground where a person stands to get in, facing the door); anchor_door_rear (on the ground behind the rear doors, facing them) where there are rear doors; anchor_cargo (centre of the load floor); anchor_light_head_l/r and anchor_light_tail_l/r; anchor_exhaust_1; anchor_camera (chase camera target, about roof height at the centre); anchor_plate_f and anchor_plate_r (centre of the licence plate recess, facing outward; the game paints plates); anchor_decal_side_l and anchor_decal_side_r (centre of the largest flat side panel, facing outward: the game paints liveries, company names and route boards there; give the usable width and height as extras); anchor_siren on the ambulance and SWAT van; anchor_sign_front on the bus (the destination board).
- Materials by role: paint for the body colour (recoloured by the game, with clear coat); paint_secondary for a second livery colour (the ambulance band, the box body, the bus skirt); glass for windows and transparent lamp lenses; lamp_head, lamp_tail, lamp_brake, lamp_reverse, lamp_indicator_l, lamp_indicator_r; lamp_siren_a and lamp_siren_b on the ambulance and SWAT van; lamp_sign on the bus destination board. Everything else bakes into the atlas with ambient occlusion.
- Colliders: boxes that follow the shape (for example col_box_cab, col_box_body, col_box_bed), at most four per vehicle; no wheel colliders.
- Levels of detail: lod0 about 12,000 to 18,000 triangles (the bus up to 22,000), lod1 about 4,000 to 5,000, lod2 about 800 to 1,400 with no interior and simple wheels. The body mesh of lod0 should be reasonably evenly tessellated (no edge longer than about 0.5 m on these big panels) because the game deforms it in crashes.
- Size: at most about 520 kB per vehicle and 3.6 MiB for all seven; set ASSET['budget'] accordingly.

In the registry, document for each vehicle its nodes, anchors, materials and the wheel radius and track the physics needs. In your report give a table of wheel radius, wheelbase, track width and seat count per vehicle.
