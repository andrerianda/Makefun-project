You are a prop artist on MARAZUL. Read /tmp/marazul/briefs/ART-RULES.md in full first: it gives the project, the rules you must not break, the pipeline and what to report. Then read art/README.md and the two existing prop scripts, art/blender/assets/props/street_lamp_a.py and newspaper_box_a.py: they were written by the pipeline's author as examples and are now yours to keep, improve or replace.

## Your job: street furniture and urban props
Model, bake and ship the objects that make streets read as a lived-in city rather than an empty model: they stand in the foreground of every on-foot and driving screenshot, the player's car knocks them over, and at night their lamps light the road. Each must look like the real manufactured thing with its construction visible (bases, brackets, bolts, seams, hinges, grilles, glass, labels as blank panels) and believable wear (chipped paint, rust at joints, grime at the foot, sun-faded plastic), at real dimensions.

You own: art/blender/assets/props/** (asset scripts and your underscore-prefixed shared helpers), game/assets/props/**, game/src/assets/registry/props.js. Scratch: /tmp/marazul/props/. Another artist is making plants, beach and leisure objects in art/blender/assets/vegetation/ and art/blender/assets/leisure/; building artists make facades, rooftop equipment, shipping containers and fences; do not make those.

## What to ship
To keep the file count low (the whole game has a hard limit on files), group related objects into set files: one asset per set, each object a named node in it with its own levels of detail, anchors and collider proxies, all sharing the set's atlas. Name sets set_<theme> and objects plainly (for example traffic_signal_mast, bench_slat). If the pipeline's naming of levels, anchors and colliders needs a per-object prefix or suffix to keep objects apart inside one file, choose a clear scheme, apply it everywhere and document it at the top of the registry, because the assembler and the physics read objects by name. Record in each object's extras (or failing that in the registry) its class for the game: fixed (never moves: big poles, shelters), knockable (breaks from its base and tumbles when a car hits it: signs, bins, hydrants, meters, small lamps, cones, boxes) with a mass in kilograms, or scatter (light debris).
1. set_street_lighting: three or four lamp designs for different districts (the existing arm lamp refined; a tall double-arm avenue lamp; a decorative promenade lamp with a globe or lantern for the beachfront; a wall-mounted or short post-top lamp for alleys and parks), each with a lamp_main emissive part and anchor_light pointing where the light goes; a utility pole with cross-arm, insulators, a transformer can and a street-light arm (the game strings the wires between anchor_wire_1..3).
2. set_traffic_control: a mast-arm traffic signal with two heads over the road and one on the post (lamp_red, lamp_amber, lamp_green roles), a post-top signal, a pedestrian signal with push button (lamp_walk, lamp_stop), sign posts with blank faces in the common shapes (octagon, triangle, round, rectangle, diamond; the game paints the faces: give each anchor_sign with width and height), a street-name post with two blades, a large overhead gantry sign frame for the freeway, a parking meter, bollards (steel and concrete), a guard-rail segment, a jersey barrier, a water-filled plastic barrier, a traffic cone, a barrel drum with stripes, a folding work barricade with a lamp.
3. set_sidewalk: benches (slatted steel-and-wood and a concrete one), litter bins (two designs), a fire hydrant, a mailbox, the newspaper box, a pay phone kiosk, a bus shelter with glazed panels, bench and blank advert panel, a bike rack with a bicycle locked to it, a drinking fountain, a planter box and a round concrete planter (empty soil top; plants come from the other artist), tree grates, a manhole cover and a storm drain as thin objects, a parking ticket machine, a street clock on a post.
4. set_commerce: vending machines (drinks and snacks, with lamp_panel), an ice freezer chest, an automatic teller machine, cafe tables and chairs, a closed and an open parasol, a sandwich-board sign and a rotating sign stand with blank faces (anchor_sign), a hot-dog cart and a fruit stall with crates of produce, a shopping trolley, stacked plastic chairs, a propane cage, an outdoor clothes rack.
5. set_alley_and_yard: dumpsters (lids open and closed), wheelie bins, piles of bin bags, cardboard boxes (single and stacked), wooden pallets (single and stacked), crates, oil drums (clean and rusty), tyre stacks, gas cylinders, a broken sofa, a mattress, scaffold frames with planks, a cement mixer, a portable toilet, a skip, a generator, cable reels, a stack of pipes, a rubble heap.
6. set_roadside: fuel pumps and the pump island with canopy columns (for fuel stations), an air-and-water machine, a car-wash sign frame, large billboard structures (a roadside one on a steel monopole and a rooftop one on a truss, both with blank faces of 12 by 4 m and 6 by 3 m, anchor_sign and lamp_flood lights along the edge), a motel-style pylon sign frame with blank panels, a bus-stop pole, a toll or security booth with a barrier arm (a separate node, barrier_arm, hinged), a speed camera pole, a radio mast segment with a beacon (lamp_beacon).
Levels of detail: lod0 as the object needs (a few hundred to 2,500 triangles; small things much less), lod1 at about a quarter, and for anything that appears by the hundred along roads (lamps, signals, signs, poles, bins, hydrants, bollards) a very cheap lod2. Colliders: simple proxies (a cylinder for a pole, boxes otherwise), one to three per object. Glass role for real glazing (shelter panels, phone kiosk, vending fronts) only. Size: at most about 480 kB per set (one 2048 atlas each, or 1024 where it is enough) and 2.8 MiB for all six; set ASSET['budget'] with the reason (a set in one file).

Build set_street_lighting and set_traffic_control first (they are in every street picture), check them in Cycles and in the engine viewer at night as well as in low sun, then the others. In the registry, list every object with its node names, class, mass, anchors, colliders and lamp roles; that table is what the city assembler, the physics and the lighting programmers work from. Repeat it per set in your report.
