## Your job: the modular facade kits of the low and mid-rise city
The city is about 3.4 by 3.0 km with thousands of buildings, so buildings are not modelled one by one: a later programmer assembles each building on its lot from facade modules you make, instancing them by the thousand. You design and bake the kits for the streets people walk and drive along, which fill most of every screenshot: pastel stucco and art-deco hotels and apartment blocks of a sun-soaked Gulf-coast resort city, shopfront streets, low-rise barrio blocks, and houses. In low golden sunlight the facades must show real depth: recessed windows with frames, sills, mullions and reveals, shutters and louvres, balconies with railings and slab edges, projecting eyebrows and fins, rounded deco corners, stepped parapets and cornices, pilasters, string courses, shopfronts with glazing, recessed doors, stall risers, fascia bands for signs and awnings, air-conditioning units, downpipes, vents, wall lamps, weathering under sills and at the base. Baked ambient occlusion and normal detail carry it at distance.

You own: art/blender/assets/buildings/** (asset scripts and your underscore-prefixed shared helpers), game/assets/buildings/**, game/src/assets/registry/buildings.js. Scratch: /tmp/marazul/buildings/. Another artist is making towers and industrial kits in art/blender/assets/towers_industry/ to the same module grid; do not write there.

## The module grid (fixed; both building artists and the assembler use it)
- A module is one bay of one storey of a facade. In Blender it is authored facing +Y (the street side), with the outer wall surface on the plane Y = 0, spanning X from -width/2 to +width/2 and Z from 0 to its height. Parts may project up to 1.6 m toward the street (awnings, balconies, canopies) and recess up to 0.7 m into the building. The back is open; the assembler closes buildings with plain walls and roofs.
- Widths: 4 m (standard bay), 2 m (narrow bay), 8 m (wide shopfront or garage bay). The assembler may stretch a module by up to 12 percent in width to fit a frontage. Heights: ground storey 4.2 m for commercial and 3.2 m for residential; upper storeys 3.2 m; top pieces (parapets, cornices, roof edges) 1.2 m, also at widths 4 and 2.
- Corners: a corner module is a square post 0.5 by 0.5 m in plan, one storey high (4.2, 3.2 and 1.2 m), with its two street faces on X = +0.25 and Y = 0, placed where two facades meet; give each style a square and, where the style calls for it, a rounded deco variant of 2 m radius that replaces the end bays of both facades.
- Slots (the vocabulary the assembler understands): ground_shop, ground_shop_wide, ground_entrance (the building's main door), ground_wall (windowed or blank ground storey), ground_garage, upper_window, upper_balcony, upper_blank, top_parapet, top_cornice, corner_ground, corner_upper, corner_top, and roof_prop (free-standing roof and wall equipment: air-conditioning condensers, water tanks, stair-head boxes, satellite dishes, antennas, skylights, vent stacks, rooftop billboards' frames, solar panels; origin at the centre of the base).
- Naming: each kit is one asset, kit_<style>; each module in it is a node named <slot>_<width in metres>_<variant letter>, for example upper_window_4_a, with its levels as children or suffixes exactly as the pipeline's level convention requires (read art/README.md). Record each module's width, height and slot in its glTF extras if the pipeline lets you, and in any case in the registry entry.
- Levels: lod0 is the detailed module (about 150 to 450 triangles for a bay, up to 900 for a shopfront). lod1 is essential, because a street shows hundreds of bays and the skyline thousands: at most 12 triangles, a flat or nearly flat card on the wall plane whose texture is baked from the detailed module (base colour, normal, occlusion, roughness, metalness and the window glow), so that the far city still shows windows with depth.
- Materials by role, so that every building can have its own colours: paint_wall for the main wall finish (baked as neutral light stucco with its texture, occlusion and weathering, tinted per building by the game), paint_trim for the accent (bands, frames, shutters, awnings, doors), lamp_window_a, lamp_window_b and lamp_window_c for window panes: the game lights a random share of them at night by group, and by day they must read as reflective glass (metalness near 1, roughness under 0.1, a mid-tone base colour: near-black glass reflects nothing) with a hint of blinds or curtains behind some; spread the three groups evenly over the modules. Shopfront glazing is glass role only where seeing in matters (then put a simple baked shop interior behind it: shelves, counters, a back wall); otherwise treat it like windows. Everything else bakes into the kit's atlas. No lettering anywhere: fascia bands and sign boxes stay blank; give each shopfront and entrance an anchor, anchor_sign_<module name>, at the centre of its sign area facing the street with the usable width and height as extras, and each wall lamp an anchor_light.
- Each kit also carries the repeating tile materials the assembler needs to close the building: tile_wall (the same finish as paint_wall, for side and back walls; say in the registry how to tint it), tile_roof_flat (gravel or membrane), and where the style has pitched roofs tile_roof_pitched (clay barrel tiles or shingles), each about 2 m square at 512 pixels.

## The kits to ship (asset ids)
1. kit_deco_resort: streamlined pastel art-deco hotels and apartment blocks of the beach district, 3 to 8 storeys: rounded corners, eyebrows over windows, vertical fins and stepped centre features, porthole windows, glass-block panels, curved balconies with pipe railings, ziggurat parapets, neon-tube channels along the edges (as a lamp_neon role).
2. kit_downtown_commercial: stucco, brick and stone-trimmed shop and office buildings of 2 to 6 storeys: generous shopfronts with awnings and roller shutters, entrance doors with canopies, sash and casement windows with sills, pilasters, a strong cornice, fire escapes on some upper modules, service doors.
3. kit_barrio_lowrise: modest painted-block and stucco buildings of 1 to 3 storeys in strong colours: small shops with security grilles and hand-built canopies, louvred windows with bars, external stairs, balconies with wrought-iron railings, flat roofs with parapets, exposed conduits and water tanks.
4. kit_residential: houses and small apartment buildings: porches with posts, front doors, picture and louvre windows, garage doors, car ports, eaves and gable ends for pitched clay-tile roofs (provide gable_end_4_a and eave_4_a top modules), stucco chimneys, plus a grander variant for the hill villas (arched loggias, columns, balustrades, french doors).
Each kit: 30 to 45 modules, one 2048 atlas (shared by all its modules) plus its tile sets, at most about 1.15 MiB, 4.6 MiB for all four; set ASSET['budget'] with the reason (a whole kit in one file). Build kit_deco_resort first and prove it by assembling, in a scratch Blender scene and in the engine viewer if it can show it, a five-storey corner building from its modules with a tint applied, lit by a low warm sun; iterate until that picture looks like a real building, then build the others the same way.

In the registry, list every module with slot, width and height, the anchors, and the material roles; that table is the assembler's only documentation. Repeat the list of modules per kit in your report, with the paths of the assembled-building pictures.
