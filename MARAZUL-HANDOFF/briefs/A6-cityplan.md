## Conventions (the same ones CONVENTIONS.md states)
1 unit = 1 metre; +Y up; -Z is north, +X is east; sea level is y = 0; angles in radians; seconds. Modern ES modules, JSDoc on exports, no frameworks. Each file has one clear job and is named for it, and its first declaration is its main export with a descriptive name. Files of a few hundred lines rather than giants. Deterministic: no Math.random and no Date; all randomness comes from a seeded generator passed in.

The seeded generator is written by another agent at game/src/core/random/seededRandom.js; its main export is a function named createSeededRandom that takes a seed and returns an object with the methods next (float in [0,1)), range(min, max), int(min, max inclusive), chance(probability), pick(array), weightedPick(items, weights), shuffle(array in place), gaussian(mean, deviation) and fork(label) (an independent generator derived from the seed and the label). Import it from there if the file exists when you need it; until then, and in your Node tests, a small equivalent of your own inside tools/tests/plan/ is fine. Your generator's functions take the generator object as a parameter rather than importing it.

## The city (fixed decisions shared with the whole team)
Marazul is a fictional Gulf-coast metropolis. Land spans roughly 3.4 km east to west and 3.0 km north to south, centred near the origin; open sea lies to the south and east; a river, the Rio Tamarindo, runs down the west side to an estuary in the south-west. Nine districts, with these exact ids:
- playa_dorada: the beach strip along the south coast: a wide sand beach of about 2 km, an ocean-front boulevard with a palm median, pastel art deco hotels, a promenade, a long pier.
- centro: downtown, north of the beach strip in the middle: a tight grid with some one-way streets, glass towers, a central plaza, the bank, city hall, the police headquarters.
- la_marisma: the old town, west of Centro: irregular narrow streets, a market square, a church, low colourful shops.
- puerto_tamarindo: the container port on the estuary in the south-west: quays, crane rails, container yards, warehouses, a shipyard.
- barrio_hierro: industry and rail yards north of the port along the river: a coarse grid, factories, a scrapyard, a rail spur and sidings.
- las_lomas: hills and villas in the north-east, rising to about 45 m: curving roads, switchbacks, cul-de-sacs, a lookout, a radio mast.
- bahia_vista: the marina and casino resort in a bay on the south-east: docks with slips, a yacht club, the casino hotel, a promenade.
- isla_coral: an island of mansions offshore to the south-east, reached by a causeway bridge of about 400 m: a loop road, gated drives, a beach club, a helipad.
- los_pantanos: wetlands at the north-west edge beyond the highway: mangroves and water channels, a few rural and dirt roads, a trailer park, a small airstrip.
A through highway skirts the north and crosses the river on a large bridge; six to eight avenues (four to six lanes, some with medians) tie the districts together; local streets fill in with a pattern that fits each district. Everything is single level: no road passes over another road (bridges cross water only), so a height field and one road layer describe the whole world. Mostly a flat coastal plain 2 to 4 m above the sea; the beach slopes into the water; Las Lomas rises smoothly.

Landmarks and points of interest are data-driven. The generator takes a list of requests (id, district id, kind, footprint class small up to 20 m, medium up to 40 m, large up to 80 m, huge beyond that, and optional needs such as coastal, on an avenue, on a corner, secluded) and places each on a suitable lot, returning its lot, entrance point on the street and facing. Ship a built-in default list so the generator works alone, covering at least these kinds across sensible districts: police_station (three, with the headquarters in centro), hospital (two), fire_station, city_hall, courthouse, bank (two), casino_resort, grand_hotel (three on the beach), motel, shopping_mall, nightclub (three), diner (three), bar_cantina (three), gun_shop (three), clothing_store (three), convenience_store (four), fuel_station (five), car_dealership, paint_and_body_shop (three), mod_garage (two), scrapyard, pier, marina, yacht_club, lighthouse, stadium, church, market, plaza, park (four), bus_terminal, train_yard, container_terminal, shipyard, airstrip, helipad (two), safehouse_apartment (three), beach_house, mansion (three), trailer_park, warehouse (six), factory (two), radio_tower, lookout. The story team will later pass its own list with its own ids and names using the same kinds, and names for streets per district; where no names are given, generate neutral invented ones.

## What to build
A deterministic generator that turns a seed and options into the complete plan of Marazul, in well under half a second, plus fast queries over it. Design the data model yourself and document it; it must include:
1. Terrain and water: the coastline, river, bay, island and wetland channels as polygons; a height field sampled on a regular grid with fast bilinear lookup (a function for the height at any x, z and one for the surface normal), conformed to roads and lots so that roads are smooth and lots are level pads; beach, sand, grass, dirt, paved and water surface types queryable at a point.
2. The road network as a graph: intersections and road segments as smooth polylines (sampled at most every 10 m, with widths, lane counts per direction, medians, parking lanes, sidewalk widths, speed limits, a class such as highway, avenue, street, alley, service, dirt or bridge, a street name and a district). Curves must be real curves in Las Lomas, the island and the highway ramps, not a grid everywhere. Intersections carry their control (signals with phase data on avenue crossings, stop signs elsewhere), their corner geometry and crosswalks.
3. Blocks and lots: the land between roads cut into lots with a frontage edge and facing, an area, a district, a zone (hotel, tower, retail, mixed_use, residential_low, villa, industrial, port_yard, civic, park, plaza, parking, beach, wetland and so on), a suggested number of floors and a style hint, without overlaps and never on a road; special areas (the beach, promenade, pier, marina slips, quay and crane rails, container yards, rail tracks, runway, parks and plazas, parking lots with their bays).
4. Navigation: a directed lane graph for traffic (lane centre lines, successors through each intersection with the turn they make, neighbours for lane changes, speed limits, which signal governs them), curbside and parking-bay spawn points with headings, a pedestrian graph (both sidewalks of every street, crosswalks, plaza, promenade and beach paths), and boat lanes along the coast and the river.
5. Districts as polygons with a fast lookup of the district, street name, lot and surface at any point; the nearest lane to a point with its projection; random points for spawning by kind; and a spatial index over everything (a uniform grid of sectors of about 128 m listing the roads, lots and areas in each) for streaming and proximity queries.
6. A 2D painter that draws the plan to a canvas context for a given view and style (land, sand, water, parks, roads by class, bridges, building lots, landmarks by kind, district labels), written so the HUD minimap and the full-screen map can reuse it later, and usable from the preview page.
7. Statistics for the report: total road length, number of intersections, lots by zone and by district, landmark placements.

## Quality bar
The plan must read as a designed city, not noise: a clear hierarchy of roads, districts with distinct textures, a believable waterfront, sensible lot sizes (hotels and towers on big lots, shops in rows, villas with gardens), landmarks where they belong, no dead-end lanes except deliberate cul-de-sacs (which loop back), no sliver lots, no roads through water except bridges, gentle gradients on roads (at most about 12 percent). Write Node tests under tools/tests/plan/ (run with `node --test`) that check these invariants on the default seed and on a few other seeds: the lane graph is connected so traffic can reach nearly every lane from any other, every lane has a successor, lots do not overlap roads or each other, every requested landmark is placed in its district, heights are continuous along roads and across lot pads, the sidewalk graph is connected, generation is deterministic (the same seed gives the same digest) and fast. Then write tools/preview/city-plan.html, which generates the default plan and paints it large with the 2D painter (and can show overlays for lanes, the pedestrian graph and zones through query parameters), capture it with the shared tool, look at the image, criticise it as a city planner would, and improve the generator. Do at least four rounds of render, critique and improve; the last rounds should be about beauty and believability, not only correctness.
