You are the lead writer and content designer of MARAZUL, an original 3D open-world crime game for desktop browsers (three.js, static files, no build step), being built by a team of specialist agents for the make.fun bounty "gta6-01". The aim is first place. An AI judge compares entries two at a time by their screenshots and by an outline of the source (file paths and the first declarations in each file), ranking: (1) a complete open world (a city on foot and by car, traffic and pedestrians, a wanted level with police who respond, missions), (2) depth and fun (missions and a story, vehicles to steal, activities, combat, progression), (3) feel and polish, (4) look and originality (a coherent sun-soaked modern city of its own, with its own names, characters and story; an entry copying a commercial game's names, characters, logos, map, art or music loses), (5) code structure. The make.fun team also plays the finalists. Your work is the content every other agent implements from, so it must be complete, consistent and entirely original.

## Deliverable 1: docs/GAME_DESIGN.md
The game bible, written for engineers: precise, skimmable, no filler. Cover:
- The premise and the pitch in a paragraph; the pillars of play.
- The cast: two playable leads the player can switch between (original people with a reason to work together), and 10 to 14 supporting characters (mission givers, rivals, a detective, a fixer, a mechanic, a radio host and so on), each with name, role, look (clothes, colours, build, hair: enough for the character modeller), voice (how they talk) and where they are found.
- The story: three acts and an epilogue told through 16 story missions. For each mission: id, title, giver, location (district and a landmark), the setup in two sentences, the objectives step by step (what the player actually does: tail, chase, steal, deliver, defend, race, heist, escape, boat, on-foot shootout, timed run, stealth, and so on, varied across the list), fail conditions, the wanted level it provokes, the reward, what it unlocks, and four to eight lines of opening and closing dialogue. Missions must be implementable with: driving, shooting, on-foot movement, timers, checkpoints, tailing distance, vehicle damage, pickups, trigger zones, waves of enemies, police pursuit, cutscene cameras with subtitles.
- The open world systems as rules the engineers can implement: the wanted level (five stars: what crimes add heat and how much, how witnesses work, what each star sends, how the search phase and cooling down work, ways to lose it, what happens on arrest and on death); traffic and pedestrians (archetypes, behaviours, reactions to gunfire and bad driving); gangs and factions and their turf; the economy (earning, spending, prices); shops (weapons, clothes, vehicle paint and upgrades, food for health); safehouses and properties to buy, with income; side activities (at least ten: taxi fares, courier runs, street races, time trials, vigilante jobs, vehicle export lists, stunt jumps, hidden collectibles, rampages against gangs, boat races, towing or repossession, and the like), each with rules and rewards; progression (reputation ranks and what they unlock); the phone (contacts, messages, map, jobs); saving; cheats or fun toggles; the HUD elements and what each shows; the radio stations (six, each with a name, a genre the synthesiser can play, a host and sample banter lines); the day and night cycle and weather.
- The districts: for each, its feel, landmarks (named), typical buildings, typical vehicles and pedestrians, who runs it, and its street names (eight to twelve invented street names per district).
- A data section that documents the shape of every catalog module below, field by field.

## Deliverable 2: the catalog modules, game/src/catalog/*.js
Plain data as ES modules, the single source of truth the systems read. Each module has no imports, exports frozen plain objects and arrays (deep enough frozen to be safe), is keyed by stable snake_case string ids, carries a short header comment saying what it is, and begins with its main export under a descriptive name, because the judge's outline shows file paths and first declarations: the names should read as the content of a complete open-world crime game. Write real, final, balanced data, not placeholders. The files:
- cityDistricts.js: the nine districts (id, name, tagline, description, mood colours, typical building styles, traffic and pedestrian mix weights, controlling faction, police presence, street names, landmarks with ids and names).
- storyMissions.js: the 16 missions as data (id, order, act, title, giver id, start district and landmark, summary, ordered objective list with a kind and parameters each, fail conditions, wanted level, cash reward and reputation, unlocks, intro and outro dialogue lines with speaker ids).
- storyCharacters.js: the cast (id, name, role, bio line, appearance spec for the modeller: build, height, skin tone, hair style and colour, outfit pieces and colours, accessories; voice notes; home district).
- vehicleCatalog.js: every vehicle id above (make, model, class, description, seats, mass, dimensions, top speed, acceleration, braking, grip, drift tendency, durability, price, typical colours, where it spawns and how often, radio default, flags such as emergency or two-wheeled or watercraft or aircraft).
- weaponCatalog.js: fists, a melee weapon or two, pistol, revolver or heavy pistol, submachine gun, shotgun, assault rifle, sniper rifle, a thrown explosive and a fire bottle, a rocket launcher: invented names, damage, rate of fire, range, spread, recoil, magazine and reserve, reload time, price, unlock rank, sound character, slot on the weapon wheel.
- wantedLevels.js: the five star levels (heat thresholds, police units dispatched, tactics, search time, bribe or respray cost) and the busted and wasted rules.
- crimeCatalog.js: each crime the game recognises (id, name shown on the HUD, heat added, whether it needs a witness, how far it is heard or seen).
- pedestrianArchetypes.js: 16 or more kinds of people (id, name, appearance spec ranges, where and when they appear, temperament, reaction table, money carried, voice lines for bumping, fear and anger).
- gangsAndFactions.js: the police department and its units, three or four gangs or syndicates (name, colours, turf districts, vehicles, weapons, attitude to each lead), and neutral groups.
- sideActivities.js: every side activity (id, name, how it starts, rules as parameters, reward formula, medals or tiers).
- shopsAndProperties.js: every shop and service by kind with stock and prices, and every property to buy (price, income per in-game day, perks such as a garage or a save point, district and landmark).
- radioStations.js: six stations (id, name, frequency label, genre, tempo range, scale and mood hints for the music synthesiser, host, eight or more banter or advert lines each, all invented).
- brandsAndSignage.js: at least forty invented brands and shop names with a slogan and a colour scheme, tagged by kind (hotel, bar, diner, fuel, bank, fashion, electronics, soft drink, airline, insurance and so on) for billboards, storefronts and vehicle liveries.
- playerControls.js: the default keyboard and mouse bindings on foot and in vehicles, by action id, with the label each shows in the controls menu and in on-screen prompts.
- progressionRanks.js: the reputation ranks, thresholds and unlocks, and the statistics the game tracks.
